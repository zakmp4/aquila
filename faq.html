How It Works

The process

We combine acoustic listening equipment, thermal imaging, moisture meters and pressure testing. Each method rules areas in or out — acoustic gear hears water escaping under pressure, thermal imaging shows where it's travelling, moisture readings trace the wettest path, and pressure testing confirms which line is at fault. By the time we mark a spot, several independent methods agree on it.

Detection itself is non-invasive — that's the entire point. The repair that follows may need access, but because we pinpoint the location first, that access is small and targeted rather than exploratory. The difference is usually one neat cut instead of a trench.

Most residential inspections are done in a single visit. Larger properties, buried water mains and complex slab leaks can take longer. We'll give you a realistic time frame when you book rather than an optimistic one.

Ideally yes, at least at the start — you know the property and what you've been noticing, and that context genuinely helps. If you can't be there, we can arrange access with an agent or tenant and walk you through the findings afterwards.

Sometimes the answer is that there isn't a leak — the moisture is condensation, ground water, or a roof and guttering issue rather than plumbing. We'll tell you that plainly. Ruling a leak out with evidence is a legitimate result, and it's a lot cheaper than a plumber opening walls on a hunch.

Your Property

Leaks & warning signs

Turn off every tap and water-using appliance, then watch your water meter. If the dial still moves, water is escaping somewhere. Other common signs: an unexplained bill spike, damp patches or bubbling paint, mould returning after cleaning, the sound of running water with everything off, warm spots on the floor, or pooling water outside when it hasn't rained.

It's a leak in the plumbing running beneath a concrete slab. It matters because the water has nowhere obvious to go, so it saturates the ground under your foundation and works its way up through the slab. Left long enough it causes movement, cracking and floor damage — and the water bill climbs the whole time.

Usually not, at least not to diagnose it. We test the shower base, the waste and the supply lines separately to identify which one is failing.
